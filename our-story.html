Our Story

Suchona through the years

  1. 1985

    The Early Roots

    Bengali families in Iowa began gathering for Saraswati Puja, Bijoya celebrations, music, dance, and small cultural performances. These early gatherings laid the foundation for a growing cultural community.

  2. 1997

    The Name “Suchona” Is Born

    As the Bengali community continued to grow, members came together to create a more formal organization. During a summer gathering, the name Suchona was chosen to represent this new beginning.

  3. 1998

    Suchona Is Founded

    Suchona was formally established in Iowa to bring together the Bengali community and promote Bengali, Indian, and South Asian culture, art, and community welfare. A constitution and first governing body helped shape the organization's future.

  4. 1998

    Iowa's First Durga Puja

    After bringing a Durga idol from Minneapolis to Iowa, the community came together to celebrate its first Durga Puja in Cedar Rapids—beginning a tradition that would become central to Suchona.

  5. 2000

    A Tradition Takes Root

    Durga Puja grew into a recurring annual celebration, bringing together families from Iowa City, Cedar Rapids, Ames, Bettendorf, Moline, and surrounding communities.

  6. 2001

    Culture Takes Center Stage

    Music, dance, drama, and literature became an increasingly important part of Suchona's identity. In 2001, acclaimed Bengali poet Joy Goswami joined the Durga Puja celebration as an honored guest.

  7. 2019

    A New Chapter

    With a new Durga idol and a new generation of members carrying the tradition forward, Suchona entered a fresh chapter of celebration, culture, and community.

  8. 2023

    25 Years of Suchona

    Suchona celebrated 25 years of bringing people together through culture, tradition, service, and shared community—honoring the members, families, volunteers, and generations who helped build its story.

  9. Today

    The Story Continues

    Suchona continues to bring generations together while looking toward the future. Through cultural celebrations, community engagement, and the energy of new members, Suchona hopes to preserve Bengali heritage, welcome new voices, and carry its traditions forward for generations to come.
